Quantum encoding (also called embedding) converts classical data into quantum states so a quantum computer can process it. This step matters because it determines how well the relationships in the data are preserved. It also maps the data into a high-dimensional Hilbert space that may be difficult to work with classically. Encoding can also be a bottleneck, since preparing quantum states can be costly in time and circuit resources.  
  
This guide will go through the four most common methods of encoding using both theory and methods implemented in the new SAS Quantum Lab. The SAS Quantum Lab lets you build and run quantum circuits, including a range of encoding methods. These workloads can run on simulators or supported quantum hardware.
