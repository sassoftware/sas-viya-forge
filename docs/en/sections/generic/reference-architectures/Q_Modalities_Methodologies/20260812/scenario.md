### Modality, information carrier, control, and computational model

| Modality | Information carrier | Typical control | Common model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral atoms | Selected atomic states | Lasers and optical tweezers | Analog and gate-based |
| Trapped ions | Electronic or hyperfine states of ions | Lasers, RF fields, and electrode voltages | Gate-based |
| Semiconductor spins | Electron or hole spin states | Microwave, magnetic, and electric control | Gate-based |
| Diamond NV centers | Electron and nearby nuclear spins | Optical and microwave control | Gate-based and networking |
| Photonics | Discrete photons or optical modes | Sources, interferometers, phase control, and detectors | Sampling, measurement-based, and gate-based |
| Superconducting circuits | Quantized circuit energy states | Microwave and flux control | Gate-based and annealing |
| Topological approaches | Nonlocal encoded quantum states | Proposed topological operations | Gate-based, proposed |

### Principal strengths and challenges

| Modality | Principal strength | Principal challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral atoms | Reconfigurable arrays and strong Rydberg interactions | Atom loss, calibration, and high-fidelity control |
| Trapped ions | High-fidelity operations and strong connectivity | Gate speed and system-level scaling |
| Semiconductor spins | Small device size and semiconductor compatibility | Uniform fabrication and control at scale |
| Diamond NV centers | Long-lived spin states and optical interfaces | Scalable coupling and fabrication consistency |
| Photonics | Low decoherence in transmission | Optical loss and resource overhead |
| Superconducting circuits | Fast operation and mature control stack | Cryogenics, noise, and limited coherence |
| Topological approaches | Potential intrinsic protection from local noise | Experimental realization and validation |
